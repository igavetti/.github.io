About Me

I am originally from northern Italy and I am currently pursuing a PhD in Neuroendocrinology at the University of Lille. My background spans Cognitive Science and Artificial Intelligence, with a focus on computational neuroscience. My goal is to contribute to advancing our understanding of brain function through computational tools.

My academic journey has provided me with a blend of interdisciplinary skills, research experience, and practical knowledge. Below are key factors which I believe would make me a strong PhD candidate:

1. Research Projects

Throughout my master’s, I have been involved in several research projects that enriched my computational and analytical skills. For example, my first research project at Radboud University (NL) explored interpretable functional-genetic models of the zebrafish brain, working with data collected through light-sheet microscopy. In the continuation of this project, I will additionally get hands-on experience in the Zebrafish facility at Radboud University, learning how the neural data is collected.

2. Literature Thesis

My literature review, titled Graph Structures in Self-Supervised Learning for EEG Data: A Review of Methodological Approaches, has deepened my understanding of EEG data as well as self-supervised learning. In this review, I touched upon graph theory and machine learning topics, in order to assess the most insightful approach to build graph structures from EEG data. I was supervised by Prof. Marijn van Wingerden.

3. Interdisciplinarity

My current Master's program has encouraged me to take an interdisciplinary approach to research, allowing me to merge knowledge from (computational) neuroscience, neurobiology, and artificial intelligence. This interdisciplinary foundation is preparing me to tackle problems in neuroscience from multiple angles. Furthermore, the completion of my master’s program requires involvement in all steps of the research cycle, spanning from literature review, to experimental design, data collection, as well as data analysis.

4. Extracurricular Activities

Outside of coursework, I am actively involved in extracurricular activities, such as contributing to the Amsterdam Brain and Cognition Journal. Through this involvement, my goal is to strengthen my writing skills, learn about the peer-review process, and contribute towards open-access science.
