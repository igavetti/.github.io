About Me

I am a PhD student in Neuroendocrinology at the University of Lille, working under the supervision of Dr. Konstantina Chachlaki.

My academic background bridges cognitive science, artificial intelligence, and computational neuroscience. My PhD focuses on the interaction between neuroinflammatory and neuroendocrine processes following preterm birth in a preclinical context, and how computational models can help disentangle these mechanisms.

Research & Teaching Experience

  • Master Research Projects — Donders Institute (NL)
    Investigated how PyTorch-based neural networks extract features to forecast behavioral patterns within the zebrafish brain, by leveraging both functional and genetic data. In this role, I acquired advanced Python skills, as well as familiarized myself with functional and genetic zebrafish data collected via light-sheet microscopy and fluorescent in-situ hybridization.
  • Teaching Assistant — University of Amsterdam (NL)
    Assisted students in the Python-based practicals for the classes Introduction to Python for Neuroscientists (from the MSc. Brain and Cognitive Sciences) and Experimentatie - Inleiding Programmeren (from the BSc. Psychobiology).
  • Research Assistant / Intern — Tilburg University (NL)
    Developed an EEG (pre)-processing pipeline to clean-up raw EEG data and conducted event-related potential, time-frequency, as well as connectivity analyses on it, employing the MNE-Python toolbox. In this role, I learned how to analyze and manipulate data in the form of NumPy arrays. I also assisted students and graded practical assignments for the labs of the class Cognitive Neuroscience. The labs spanned the topics: EEG data analysis, Decision Making, Executive Functions, Emotions and Social Cognition, Memory and Brain-Computer Interfaces, which I also contributed in designing.
